# The Good AI! (P8509)

## In one paragraph

A two-year project (**1 Nov 2025 to 31 Oct 2027**) that helps independent and local media in Indonesia, primarily **West Java**, use AI responsibly in daily work, with a focus on **environmental journalism**. Overall objective: more diversity of opinion and press freedom, by building the AI capacity of independent and local media.

## Money and structure

| Who | Role |
|---|---|

Questions go to the sub-project lead, or **Dzaki Dwitama (Jek)** at Labtek Indie.

## Why it exists

- **Press freedom is shrinking.** Media dominated by economic elites. Laws like UU ITE drive self-censorship. Independent media fill the gap with few resources.
- **AI isn't built for local contexts.** Models learn mostly from English, Western data. Local and indigenous environmental knowledge is missing.
- **Independent media are left out.** AI tools are expensive, and public debate on AI in journalism rarely goes beyond disinformation.

## Who it reaches

- 250-300 journalists, media professionals and journalism students in West Java (trainings)
- 350-400 people interested in environmental journalism, activism and AI (webinars)
- 40-50 reps of local organisations and national institutions (dialogue)
- About 3,000 people indirectly through campaigns, focus on vulnerable groups and indigenous communities in West Java. Women's equal participation supported throughout.

## Three measures (work packages)

| Code | Aim | Targets by Oct 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| **M1 Capacity Development** | Journalists gain skills to use AI in everyday reporting, esp. environment and climate | 18 in-person trainings (250-300 people), 20 webinars (350-400), 6 Open Educational Resources, 1 Learning Festival in 2027 (80-100) |
| **M2 Campaigning & Advocacy** | Local media and stakeholders support AI-based strategies; local voices reach policy | 12 journalistic publications, 4 social media campaigns, 4 advocacy events + a joint manifesto, network of 15+ organisations |
| **M3 Co-creation & Solution Transfer** | AI working models built **with** journalists, tested in real newsroom work | User research report, **4-5 AI working models**, **2-3 pilot projects**, final strategy design handed to a non-commercial consortium of local media |

P012 (my workshop) sits in **M3**. Its 15 prototypes are the raw input for M3.2a (working models) and M3.2b (pilots) in 2027. That is the "every part has its money when it becomes a running project" that Salman mentioned.

## Five principles every sub-project follows

1. **Human in the loop.** AI supports, journalists always make the final decision.
2. **Co-creation.** Built with journalists through research and iteration. No product fixed in advance.
3. **Local context first.** West Java, Bahasa Indonesia, local and indigenous knowledge.
4. **Open and sustainable.** Open-source tools, no licence fees, ownership handed to local partners.
5. **Do no harm.** Protect journalists and sources from repression and data risks.

Principles 4 and 5 are exactly why "local AI for data privacy" is one of Salman's keywords.

## Timeline

- **Nov 2025**: project starts
- **Through 2026**: AI intro classes, webinars, hackathons (M1). Microsite, social media, advocacy events (M2). User research, then ideation and co-creation workshops (M3)
- **Jan-Mar 2027**: training of trainers, then decentralised trainings in partner newsrooms (M1)
- **Rest of 2027**: pilot projects and strategy design (M3). Learning Festival and final publication (M1)
- **31 Oct 2027**: project ends, results and ownership handed to local partners

## Key terms

- **HITL (Human in the loop)**: people stay in control of AI, journalists make the final call
- **OER (Open Educational Resources)**: free, reusable learning materials in Bahasa Indonesia
- **Co-creation**: designing solutions with the people who will use them
- **Strategy design**: the plan for how local media keep using the AI models after 2027
- **Measure codes**: M1-M3 are work packages. M1.1, M3.2 etc. are activities inside them
