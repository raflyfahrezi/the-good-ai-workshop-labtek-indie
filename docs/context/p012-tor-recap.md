## Objective in one line

Turn the 7 Phase 1 pain points into **15 lo-fi prototypes** of AI-supported solutions, built **with 45 journalists** across **5 co-creation sessions** (3 teams x 5 sessions).

Three sub-objectives:
1. **Problem to idea.** Journalists pick a pain point they recognise and generate AI ideas for the right workflow stage.
2. **Make it tangible.** The strongest idea per team becomes a lo-fi prototype a journalist can react to, sped up by the AI developer and UI/UX designer in the team.
3. **Learn what's worth building.** Document each prototype, journalists' reactions and risks, so the best go forward as working models and pilots.

Indicators: M3.1a (journalistic work contexts), M3.1b (analysis report), and probably M3.2a (AI working models, target 4-5). A shortlist from the 15 feeds M3.2a and later M3.2b pilots.

## What a lo-fi prototype is (and isn't)

**Is:**
- Rough but concrete: paper sketch, clickable mock-up, scripted demo, or simple AI workflow
- Tied to a **specific pain point**, a **specific user** (journalist, editor or newsroom leader) and a **specific workflow stage**
- Good enough for a journalist to react to: **"would I use this, and where would it break?"**
- Designed with a **human in the loop**, showing where the journalist checks, corrects or decides

**Isn't:**
- Production software, a hosted service, a finished product
- A commitment to build (selection happens after Phase 2)
- A tool selection exercise or AI training programme
- **A place for real source material. Dummy or public data only.**

"We do not arrive with ideas; the teams generate them." So I should **not** show up pushing a pre-built solution. Prepared building blocks are fine, a pre-picked answer is not.

## Session flow (Double Diamond, no fixed timings)

| # | Diamond | Step | What happens | Who leads | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set-up | Opening | Welcome, purpose, form 3 teams | Lead facilitator | 3 teams |
| 2a | Problem, diverge (Discover) | Understand the pain point | Briefing on Phase 1 findings and 7 pain points. Journalists share real examples | Lead facilitator, journalists | Many lived examples |
| 2b | Problem, converge (Define) | Frame the problem | Team picks one pain point, writes "How might we help [who] with [problem] at [workflow stage]?" | Team, co-facilitator | 1 framed problem |
| 3 | Solution, diverge (Develop) | Generate ideas | Ideas for how AI could help at that stage, no early judging. **AI developer says what is feasible** | Journalists lead, **AI dev advises** | Long list of ideas |
| 4 | Solution, converge (Develop) | Choose one | Biggest difference for whoever has the problem. Card fields 1-6 | Team, co-facilitator | 1 idea, draft card |
| 5 | Solution, converge (Deliver) | **Build the lo-fi prototype** | Sketch, clickable mock-up, scripted demo, or simple AI workflow. Dummy/public data only. Card field 7 | **UI/UX designer and AI dev build, journalists steer** | 1 prototype |
| 6 | Deliver | Test with the other teams | Teams rotate, journalists from other teams try each prototype, say where it works and breaks | All journalists, co-facs take notes | Feedback |
| 7 | Deliver | Capture feedback | Card fields 8-10: feedback, risks, what to test next, workload gut-check | Co-fac with team | 3 complete cards |
| 8 | Close | Closing | Each team presents, lead facilitator wraps up | All | Overview of 3 prototypes |

Teams in the same session can pick the same pain point if they approach it differently. The lead facilitator tracks pain point coverage across sessions. If a pain point is still untaken after Session 4, Session 5 prioritises it.

| 4. The idea in one sentence | "A ___ that helps ___ to ___, so they no longer ___." |
| 5. What AI does | The task AI takes on, and which data it uses (dummy or public only) |
| 6. **Where the human decides** | Where the journalist checks, corrects or approves. **"If this is empty, the idea has a problem."** |
| 7. Sketch / demo | Photo of paper sketch, screenshot, or mock-up link |
| 8. Feedback from journalists | Would use it because... / Would break when... |
| 9. Risks | Accuracy / Source and data safety / Moves work to a later stage. Notes |
| 10. Test next | What to test or change next. **"Does it reduce TOTAL workload, not just move it to a later stage?" Yes / Unsure / No** |

## Principles, consent, confidentiality

- **Human in the loop**: every prototype shows where the journalist checks, corrects, decides
- **Built with journalists, not for them**: teams choose problems and shape ideas
- **Do no harm**: no real source material, personal data or unpublished reporting goes into prototypes **or AI tools**. Dummy or public data only
- **Informed consent**: recorded only with consent, anyone can go off the record anytime
- **Credit**: journalists and partners credited as co-creators unless they opt out
- **Open output**: cards and final report shared openly, licence TBC. The Bahasa ToR says process documentation is published publicly
- Quotes attributed to a person or newsroom only with approval. Internal newsroom info not shared without agreement. Data stored on Labtek Indie's shared drive

Practical for me: **don't paste anything a journalist shares about real sources into ChatGPT/Gemini during the build**, even "just to demo". Swap in dummy data. This is also a good live moment to show local AI.
