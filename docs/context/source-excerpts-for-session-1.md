### MUST: what a lo-fi prototype is and is not (main ToR, p4)

> We do not arrive with ideas; the teams generate them.
>
> A lo-fi prototype is:
> - a rough but concrete representation of an idea: paper sketches, a clickable mock-up, a scripted demo or a simple AI workflow
> - tied to a specific pain point, a specific user (journalist, editor or newsroom leader) and a specific stage of the editorial workflow
> - good enough for a journalist to react to: "would I use this, and where would it break?"
> - designed with a human in the loop, showing where the journalist checks, corrects or decides
>
> A lo-fi prototype is not:
> - production software, a hosted service or a finished product
> - a commitment to build: selecting what moves forward happens after Phase 2
> - a tool selection exercise or AI training programme
> - a place for real source material: prototypes use dummy or public data only

Note: "not a tool selection exercise". Don't turn the build step into "which app should we use".

### MUST: the prototype card (main ToR, p13)

This is the form my team fills in. Fields 5-7 are where I contribute most.

> **4. The idea in one sentence:** "A ___ that helps ___ to ___, so they no longer ___."
>
> **5. What AI does:** [The task AI takes on, and which data it uses: dummy or public only]
>
> **6. Where the human decides:** [Where the journalist checks, corrects or approves the output. If this is empty, the idea has a problem.]
>
> **7. Sketch / demo:** [Paste a photo of the paper sketch, a screenshot, or a link to the mock-up]
>
> **9. Risks:** ☐ Accuracy ☐ Source / data safety ☐ Moves work to a later stage
>
> **10. Test next:** [What to test or change in the next iteration] Does it reduce TOTAL workload, not just move it to a later stage? ☐ Yes ☐ Unsure ☐ No

### MUST: the principles (main ToR, p10)

> - Human in the loop. Every prototype shows where the journalist checks, corrects, or decides. AI supports; journalists make the final call.
> - Built with journalists, not for them. Teams choose the problems and shape the ideas. No solution is fixed in advance.
> - Do no harm. No real source material, personal data, or unpublished reporting goes into prototypes or AI tools. Teams use dummy or public data.

> **AI Otomatis** · Beban turun banyak, kepekaan rendah · AI menjalankan, manusia mengawasi · Kandidat utama; wajib menunjukkan cara manusia mengecek sampel hasilnya
>
> **Guided Interpretation** · Beban turun banyak, kepekaan tinggi · AI menelusuri dan menyusun, manusia menafsirkan dan memutuskan · Kandidat utama; wajib menunjukkan titik kendali manusia dan cara mengakses sumber asli
>
> **Bantuan AI** · Beban turun sedikit, kepekaan rendah · AI mempercepat, manusia merapikan · Dipilih hanya jika tidak ada ide di bagain separuh atas

## 3. Phase 1 report (`P003-report-phase-1-short.pdf`, 97 pages)

Pages 1-53 are English, pages 54-97 are the Indonesian version of the same report.

### MUST: the opening, and a difference between the two versions (p2 vs p55)

> "Apakah itu meringankan beban jurnalis? Enggak, malah kayaknya (berpotensi) mengeksploitasi Jurnalis." Iman Herdiana - Editor Bandung Bergerak
>
> "AI itu kan biasanya supaya kerjanya lebih cepat, lebih efisien, tidak memakan waktu lama. Nggak juga, sama aja kerjanya." Mawa Kresna - Editor Project Multatuli

The English commentary under these quotes (p2):

> These comments suggest that most journalists perceive AI only superficially, remaining unaware of the potential and depth of integrating AI assistance more systematically.

The Indonesian commentary (p55):

> Komentar-komentar ini mengindikasikan bahwa jurnalis memiliki kekhawatiran nyata terhadap implementasi AI di newsroom, baik itu dari sisi potensi eksploitasi terhadap jurnalis, maupun peningkatan efisiensi yang belum dirasakan secara signifikan oleh jurnalis.

Note: the English blames journalists for misunderstanding AI. The Indonesian, which is what the room reads, takes their concerns seriously. **Use the Indonesian framing.** Never say "you just don't understand AI yet."

### MUST: the implication everything builds on (p52-53)

> The goal is not to automate judgment, but to give journalists a stronger basis for giving the Decision.
>
> **Maintain the connection between the output to their sources and original context.** Transcripts, summaries, extracted data, analysis and drafts should remain linked to recordings, documents, sources, and their original context.
>
> **Show what AI changed and what remains unverified.** Journalists and editors need to see what AI changed, where each claim came from, and which parts still require verification.
>
> **Keep editorial decisions with human.** AI may organize and help produce materials. But, decisions about meaning, safety, fairness, and publication remain human responsibilities.
>
> Implication 1 Editorial accountability: Can the newsroom trace, verify, and remain accountable for the output?

> **Reduce total workload across roles.** A feature should not save time for journalists while creating additional verification, administration, or coordination for editors.
>
> **Work with the existing support (and capacity).** Adoption should not require a dedicated AI team, extensive onboarding, or continuous maintenance that the newsroom cannot provide.
>
> Implication 02 Newsroom capacity: Can the newsroom adopt, govern, and maintain it?

Note: two test questions for any idea: can they trace it, and can a small newsroom maintain it.

### MUST: workload shift and transparency (p47-48)

> AI is truly saves time when it reduces work across the editorial workflow, not just at one stage.

> "It doesn't actually eliminate the work, it just shifts it. Does it reduce the time spent? Not really. It takes about the same amount of time, and the workflow remains the same." Kresna, Editor - Project Multatuli

> AI use itself was not always the problem. Editors needed to know where it entered the process so they could decide what required closer checking.
>
> Without disclosure, editors cannot know which drafts were AI-assisted. They must first look for signs of AI use, then check and repair any problems they find.

> "When a contributor writes with AI and doesn't tell the editor, it makes the editor's job harder… If they'd said so from the start, at least we'd be prepared when we take it through the process." Kresna, Editor - Project Multatuli

Note: this is the Galih problem in the research's own words.

### MUST: the AI line newsrooms draw (p41)

> **GENERALLY ACCEPTED** · Supporting tasks · AI assisted with research, brainstorming, transcription, document reading, or data processing while journalists retained editorial control.
>
> **GREY AREA** · Assisting with drafting · Once AI began helping with producing story draft, acceptance depended on journalists reviewing, reshaping, and taking responsibility for the output.
>
> **PROBLEMATIC** · Submitting AI generated as is and no repercussion (yet) · Unedited AI output could remain empty, repetitive, overly polished, or disconnected from the journalist's intended meaning. Repairing it then became the editor's responsibility.

> "AI is fine for transcripts, translation, processing large data — but when it comes to the final product, that has to be written by a human." Joko, Editor in Chief, Bandung Bergerak

Note: ideas that write the final story will meet resistance. Ideas in "supporting tasks" land easily.

Good for "this is exactly what Mas/Mbak X said in the research" moments.

> **#1 Information discovery:** "We start by researching the initial data and mapping out the sources… we still have to determine what is relevant and what isn't." Yulia, Journalist - Mongabay

Getting back into it — the mood, the concentration — takes effort." Yulia, Journalist, Mongabay

## 4. Project handbook (`P8509-project-handbook.pdf`, 4 pages)

### GOOD TO KNOW: the principles every sub-project follows (p2)

> - Human in the loop. AI supports; journalists always make the final decision.
> - Co-creation. Built with journalists through research and iteration; no product fixed in advance.
> - Local context first. West Java, Bahasa Indonesia, local and indigenous knowledge.
> - Open and sustainable. Open-source tools, no licence fees, ownership handed to local partners.
> - Do no harm. Protect journalists and sources from repression and data risks.

Note: "Open and sustainable... no licence fees" supports the local and open-model argument for the production version, even if the demo uses a cloud model.

### GOOD TO KNOW: why the project exists (p1)

> AI is not built for local contexts. Models learn mostly from English, Western data; local and indigenous environmental knowledge is missing.
